BJJ camera setup: what you need to film your rolls and matches

4 min read8 sections
The short answer

You do not need expensive gear to film BJJ. A phone on a tall tripod at the side of the mat, recording in 1080p with a wide frame, is enough to see every pass, sweep and submission attempt. Add a power bank and enough free storage, and you have a setup that works in any academy. Auto-tracking cameras can help, but a fixed wide frame is often more reliable for grappling.

By the GrappleMind team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Phone or camera

For most people, the phone you already have is the best camera. It is always with you, and the footage is easy to upload and share with your coach.

  • Use the wide lens. On most phones the main lens is enough. On a small mat, or when you have to stand close, switch to the ultra-wide lens so the whole round stays in the frame.
  • 1080p is enough. You can see grips and hips clearly in 1080p. 4K files are several times larger and fill your phone fast, and they do not add much for match review.
  • Lock the focus and the exposure. Tap and hold on the mat before you start, so the phone does not keep refocusing when the athletes move.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb. A call or a notification can stop the recording in the middle of a round.

A dedicated action camera or a camcorder is useful if you film every session, or if you want a wider lens and a longer battery than your phone gives you.

Tripods and mounts

The tripod matters more than the camera. The camera should be a little above hip height, a step back from the mat, so one body blocks the other less often. Read more in How to film your BJJ rolls.

  • A tall tripod with a phone clamp. Pick one that reaches at least chest height, with legs that spread wide enough not to fall when someone rolls into it.
  • A clamp mount. A clamp on a pillar, a wall bar or a fence keeps the camera high and out of the way, and nobody can knock it over.
  • Keep it off the mat. Put the tripod outside the training area, and check that nobody will roll into it.

Auto-tracking cameras

Auto-tracking cameras and gimbals follow the movement for you, so the round stays in the frame even when it moves across the mat. They can work well for one-on-one rounds in an open space.

Grappling is a hard case for them: two bodies are tangled together, and other pairs roll right next to you. A tracker can lose the round or jump to the pair next to you. Before you buy one, test it in a real round at your academy. In many cases a fixed wide frame from a good spot gives you more usable footage.

Storage and power

  • Keep free space on your phone before training. A few rounds of video take a lot of space, especially in 4K.
  • Bring a power bank. Recording drains the battery fast, and a phone that dies in round three loses the round you wanted.
  • Move the footage off your phone every week, to the cloud or to your computer, and keep it by date so you can compare rounds months apart.

Sound

You do not need sound to analyze a match. A microphone helps only if your coach comments while you roll, or if you want to record the referee's calls in competition.

Filming at a competition

Ask a teammate to film your match, as close to the mat as the event allows. Some events have rules about filming from the mat area, so check before the day. Many large tournaments are streamed, and you can often find your match in the replay.

Questions people ask

What is the best camera to film BJJ?

For most people, the phone they already have. Put it on a tall tripod at the side of the mat, use the wide lens and record in 1080p. A dedicated camera helps only if you film every session or need a longer battery.

Do I need 4K to film my BJJ rolls?

No. 1080p shows grips, hips and transitions clearly, and the files are several times smaller than 4K.

Are auto-tracking cameras worth it for BJJ?

Sometimes. They follow the movement, but in grappling they can lose the round or follow another pair. Test one in a real round at your academy first. A fixed wide frame is often more reliable.

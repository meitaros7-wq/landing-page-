How to build a BJJ competition game plan

4 min read7 sections
The short answer

A good BJJ competition game plan is built around your strong areas: the places you are comfortable being in during a match, and advancing from. Your own matches are the place to see what works best for you, not just any list of techniques. Start from where your matches are actually won and lost, find the most efficient path from the start of the match to your best positions, and train that path in the weeks before the tournament.

By the Matsense team, reviewed by black belt world champion Amit Burshtein

Choose your path through the match

A game plan is a path to victory: how you start the match, the first position you look for, how you score from there, and how you keep going all the way to the finish.

Your game planSaved on this device only
  • The start. Takedown or guard pull. Go in with a plan: know it before the match, not when the referee says go.
  • On top. Your passing style, and the position you want to reach after the pass.
  • On the bottom. Your guard, and the sweep, back take or submission you want from it.
  • The finish. The two or three submissions you hit most often from your best positions.

Keep it to one main path and one backup for when the start does not go your way. Under pressure, a short plan gets used; a long one does not.

Plan for the score

Know how points and advantages are given under the ruleset you compete in, and what to do when you are ahead or behind with a minute left. Read more in IBJJF points explained.

Plan the weeks before the competition

  1. Further out. Fix the biggest gap your match analysis found, one task at a time, and keep developing yourself as a fighter.
  2. Closer to the competition. Drill your main path from the exact positions, then test it in positional sparring and in full rounds. Keep adjusting and sharpening what you built in the previous phase and the mistakes you fixed.
  3. The final days. Lighter training and no need for new techniques: mainly movement, staying sharp and healthy, with a good attitude and mindset.

Know your opponent when you can

If you can find footage of your opponent, look for how they like to start, where they score, and where they get stuck. Analyze their matches the same way you analyze your own. No fear of them: you are just getting ready and familiar with what you are going to face and what you need to do.

After the competition

Analyze the match. Did you get to your positions? Which almosts came up again? The answers are the start of your next game plan.

In Matsense, every match you analyze adds to your library. You build tasks and keep notes, and your weekly training plan comes with a streak and a countdown to your next competition. You can bring your coach in to review your matches and your plan with you.

Questions people ask

How do you build a BJJ competition game plan?

Build it around your strong areas, using your own matches to see what works best for you. Start from where your matches are actually won and lost, find the most efficient path from the start of the match to your best positions, and train that path in the weeks before the tournament.

What should a BJJ game plan include?

How you start the match (takedown or guard pull), your passing style on top, your guard and the sweep, back take or submission you want from the bottom, and the two or three submissions you hit most often. Keep one main path and one backup.

How should I train in the weeks before a BJJ competition?

Further out, fix the biggest gap your match analysis found, one task at a time. Closer to the competition, drill your main path from the exact positions and test it in positional sparring and full rounds. In the final days, train lighter, with no new techniques, and focus on staying sharp and healthy.

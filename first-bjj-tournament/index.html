Your first BJJ tournament: how to prepare and what to expect

4 min read9 sections
The short answer

Your first BJJ tournament goes better when nothing on the day is a surprise. In the weeks before, pick the right division, read the rules and train rounds that start the way a match starts. Pack the night before, arrive early, and know how weigh-in works at your event. Ask a teammate to film your matches, and watch them within a few days, win or lose.

By the GrappleMind team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Weeks before: choose your division and learn the rules

  • Pick the event and the division. Divisions are set by belt, age, weight and Gi or No-Gi. Ask your coach which events are a good first step for you.
  • Register before the deadline. Many events close registration or raise the price close to the date.
  • Read the rulebook. Know how points and advantages work, and which techniques are not allowed at your belt. Read IBJJF points explained.
  • Know your weight class, and how you are weighed. At IBJJF events you are weighed in your gi, right before your first match, so your weight with the gi is the number that counts. Other events weigh differently, so check yours.

Train for the match, not only the roll

  • Start rounds standing. Many training rounds start on the knees, but a match starts standing. Practice your first move from the feet: the takedown, the pull or the guard you want.
  • Roll in match length. Do a few rounds at the time of your division, with a partner who pushes the pace.
  • Build a simple plan. One or two ways to start, and your best positions from there. Read How to build a BJJ competition game plan.
  • Do not learn new things in the last week. Sharpen what you already do well.

The day before

  • Check the schedule and the bracket. Know when your division starts and on which mat.
  • Pack your bag the night before.
  • Sleep. A good night matters more than one more training session.
  • Do not make a big weight cut for your first tournament. Compete close to your normal weight, so you can focus on the match.

Weigh-in

Find out before the day where and when weigh-in happens. If it is right before your first match, as at IBJJF events, be ready in your gi when your division is called. If you are over your weight class, you may be disqualified, so check your weight with the gi a few days before.

What to expect on the day

  1. Arrive early. Schedules move, and divisions sometimes start earlier than planned.
  2. Warm up about 15 minutes before your call, enough to sweat but not to get tired.
  3. Listen for your name and your mat. Stay close to the area when your division is near.
  4. Between matches, eat light, drink, stay warm and watch your next opponent if you can. Read How to scout a BJJ opponent.
  5. The match itself goes fast. Start with your plan, keep breathing, and keep working until the referee stops the match.

Film every match

Ask a teammate to film every match, from as close to the mat as the event allows. Many large tournaments are also streamed. Read How to film your BJJ rolls.

After the tournament

Win or lose, your first tournament is the best footage you will have of your game under pressure. Give it a day, then watch your matches and look for the almosts and the moments that decided them. Read What to do after losing a BJJ match.

Questions people ask

How do I prepare for my first BJJ tournament?

Pick your division with your coach, read the rules, and train rounds that start standing and last as long as your matches. Build a simple plan from your best positions, and do not learn new techniques in the last week.

What should I bring to a BJJ tournament?

A gi that fits the rules and a spare, or No-Gi clothes that follow the rules, a mouthguard, a belt, flip-flops, tape, water, light food, an ID, and a charged phone for filming.

How does weigh-in work at a BJJ tournament?

It depends on the event. At IBJJF events you are weighed in your gi right before your first match. Check your event's rules, and check your weight with the gi a few days before.

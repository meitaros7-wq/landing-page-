How Matsense analyzes a BJJ match

Upload a match, and Matsense marks every guard pass, sweep, takedown, submission attempt and turning point, so your review starts where the learning is.

By the Matsense team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

The problem: rewatching instead of learning

Every competitor knows the routine. You film the match, sit down to review it, and spend most of the time waiting for something to happen. Your memory of the match is not reliable either: adrenaline makes it subjective, and the moments you remember are rarely the ones that decided it.

We call that first watch the staring phase. The real work only starts in the second phase, critical viewing: rewinding one exchange, seeing what happened, and finding the hole, the mistake or the missed opportunity.

What Matsense does: it removes the staring phase. You open a match and go straight to the moments that matter, each one marked and explained, and every insight you take from it becomes something to train.

How Matsense recognizes what happens in a match

The analysis is not a generic video model guessing at grappling. It works from a rulebook written by competitors.

1. A rulebook written by a black belt world champion

Amit Burshtein, black belt, UWW World Champion and IBJJF No-Gi European Champion, wrote precise definitions for every position and transition the engine looks for: more than 35 event types, from guard passes, sweeps, takedowns, mount, knee on belly and back takes to submission attempts, near passes, leg lock exchanges, guard pulls, scrambles, resets and penalties.

The definitions are exact on purpose. A sweep needs a real guard and real height, not just a change of top position. Danger is not a pass. An opening only counts if it actually took hold. These are the same distinctions a referee and a high-level coach make.

2. Every moment is marked, typed and attributed

A video AI model reads the whole match against that rulebook. Each event comes back with its start and end time, its type, which athlete did it, the score when the referee gave one, and a short description of what happened, so you can jump straight to it.

The engine follows a clear order of priorities: first the event, then the athlete, then the score. Identifying correctly that something happened matters most. That is why it works on imperfect footage too.

3. A second, sharper look at how it ended

Finishes happen fast. After the full pass, Matsense cuts the end of the match at full resolution and takes a focused second look: how the match ended, and who finished whom. If the two passes disagree, the moment is flagged for review instead of being guessed.

4. It learns from champions’ corrections

World-class competitors review analyzed matches and correct what the engine got wrong. Every real correction, across every reviewed match, is carried into future analyses, so the same mistake is not repeated.

What you get from it

  • No more rewatching. Every point, attempt and turning moment, marked on the match timeline.
  • A fight breakdown from your side. What worked, what to improve, the key turning points, and why you won or lost.
  • Insights that become training. Each conclusion becomes a task, sorted into folders that group similar moments across matches.
  • A plan to follow. A weekly training plan, a streak, and a countdown to your next competition.
  • A coach in the loop. Connect your own coach, or a pro coach through the app, to review your matches with you.

Which matches can you analyze?

Any match you can film.

  • Competition and training. A tournament final or a hard round at your academy.
  • Gi and No-Gi. The engine knows which one it is watching and does not invent lapel or collar grips in a No-Gi match.
  • IBJJF and other rulesets. Scores follow the referee when the match is scored, and the events are found either way.
  • Any footage. Official streams, or a phone filming from the stands, cropped or partly blocked.

Matsense marks what both athletes did. If your opponent passed your guard, you see that moment too, because that is exactly what you need to learn from.

How to analyze your BJJ matches: focus on the almosts

The moments that decide your progress are rarely the points on the scoreboard. They are the submissions you almost finished and the defenses that almost held.

By the Matsense team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Focus on the almosts

The scoreboard tells you what happened. The almosts tell you what to train.

An almost is a moment where the match nearly went another way. Those moments show the exact gap between where your game is and where it needs to be, and they are easy to miss because nothing appears on the scoreboard.

Offensive almosts

  • The submission you locked in and lost.
  • The guard pass you nearly completed before they recovered.
  • The sweep that got them off balance but did not come over.
  • The back take where you had the angle but not the hooks.

For each one, ask: what was missing to finish it? A grip, an angle, timing, or the step before.

Defensive almosts

  • The pass you tried to stop and could not.
  • The submission you defended for a while and then tapped to.
  • The escape you started and did not finish.

For each one, ask: where did the defense start too late? Usually the problem is one or two steps before the moment you remember.

Matsense marks these moments for you: submission attempts, near passes, sweep attempts, back exposure threats and other attempts, for both athletes, so none of the almosts get lost in a full rewatch.

A simple way to review a match

  1. Look at the turning points first. The few moments that decided the match.
  2. Go through every almost. Yours and your opponent’s.
  3. Watch each one twice. Once at normal speed, once slowly from a few seconds before it started.
  4. Write one conclusion per moment. Short and specific: what happened, and what you would do differently.
  5. Look for patterns. The same almost across several matches is your top priority.

How to take your conclusions from the match to the mat

Analysis only matters if it changes how you train. The way to make it stick:

  1. Turn each conclusion into one task. Not “improve my passing”, but “win the inside grip before stepping to the leg drag”.
  2. Drill it from the exact position. Start where the almost happened, not from neutral.
  3. Test it in positional sparring. Rounds that start from that position, against resistance.
  4. Put it in your weekly plan. A task with a day on it gets trained. A note in your phone does not.
  5. Check it in the next match. Did the same almost come up again, and did it finish this time?

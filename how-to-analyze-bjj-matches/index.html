How to analyze a BJJ match

4 min read5 sections
The short answer

To analyze a BJJ match, focus on two kinds of moments: the almosts that could have changed the match, and the moments that actually decided it. The scoreboard shows only part of that story. The submission you almost finished, the defense that almost saved you, and the moment your opponent got past your guard frames are what you need to study.

By the GrappleMind team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Focus on the almosts

The scoreboard sums up what happened. The almosts on top of it are what tell you what to train.

An almost is a moment where the match nearly went another way. Those moments show the exact gap between where your game is and where it needs to be, and they are easy to miss because they do not appear on the scoreboard.

Offensive almosts

  • The submission that could have ended it all, and slipped away.
  • The guard pass that was almost completed before they recovered.
  • The sweep that almost went through, and would have won the match.
  • The turning point that was missed: a moment that could have flipped the match and did not.

For each one, ask: what was missing to finish it? A grip, an angle, timing, or the step before.

Defensive almosts

  • The pass you tried to stop and could not.
  • The submission you defended for a while and then tapped to.
  • The escape you started and did not finish.

For each one, ask: what could I do differently? Usually the problem is one or two steps before the moment you remember.

The moments that decided the match

At the same time, study the moments where the move did land. They show what actually decided the match, for you or against you.

  • An athlete broke through the guard frames.
  • A sweep or reversal that turned the position around.
  • A significant score that changed the match.
  • A submission that was caught.

For each one, ask: what made it work, and what came right before? The answer is often a grip or a position that started earlier than the moment itself.

How to take your conclusions from the match to the mat

Analysis only matters if it changes how you train. The way to make it stick:

  1. Turn each conclusion into one task. Not “improve my passing”, but “win the inside grip before stepping to the leg drag”.
  2. Drill it from the exact position. Start where the almost happened, not from neutral.
  3. Test it in positional sparring. Rounds that start from that position, against resistance.
  4. Put it in your weekly plan. A task to do in a planned time gets done. A note in your phone does not.
  5. Test it in live training. Notice the change happening in real time in the training room, and keep track of your progress toward that goal.
  6. Check it in the next match. Did the same almost come up again, and did it finish this time?

Questions people ask

How do you analyze a BJJ match?

Focus on two kinds of moments: the almosts that could have changed the match, and the moments that actually decided it. Look at the turning points first, go through every almost, watch each one twice, write one conclusion per moment, and look for patterns across matches.

What is an almost in a BJJ match?

An almost is a moment where the match nearly went another way: a submission that slipped away, a guard pass that was almost completed, a sweep that almost went through. Those moments show the exact gap between where your game is and where it needs to be, and they are easy to miss because they do not appear on the scoreboard.

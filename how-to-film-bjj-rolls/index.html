How to film your BJJ rolls

5 min read7 sections
The short answer

To film your BJJ rolls, you only need a phone and a small tripod or clamp. Put the camera at the side of the mat, a little above hip height, far enough back to keep both of you in frame for the whole round. Ask your partner before you press record, film full rounds rather than highlights, and watch the footage within a day or two, while you still remember how the round felt.

By the Matsense team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Why film your rolls

After a hard round, your memory keeps one or two moments and fills in the rest. The footage shows what actually happens, not what you think from your own perspective.

  • You see the almosts. The sweep that almost went through and the pass you almost stopped. They are easy to miss from inside the round.
  • You see what came before. The problem usually starts a grip or a position before the moment you remember.
  • You see your progress. Footage from three months ago next to footage from today shows changes you cannot feel from day to day.

Where to put the camera

Camera spotTap a rule to see it on the mat
  • At the side of the mat, a step back. Far enough that you do not roll out of frame when the round moves.
  • A little above hip height, facing the round and seeing their whole bodies. From a higher angle, one body blocks the other less often, and you can see grips and hips.
  • Not facing a window or a bright light. Backlight turns both athletes into dark shapes.
  • Away from the busy area. Pick a corner where other pairs are less likely to roll in front of the camera.

Check the frame before the round starts. If you can see both athletes from head to feet, it is good enough.

What to film

  • Full rounds, not highlights. The moments that matter are often in the quiet parts of the round.
  • Rounds with partners around your level and slightly above or below it. They show real insights faster.
  • Positional rounds. When you are working on a specific position, film rounds that start from it.
  • Your competition matches. Ask a teammate to film from as close to the mat as the event allows. Many large tournaments are also streamed, and you can often find your match in the replay.

What to do with the footage

  1. Watch it within a day or two. While you still remember what you were trying to do.
  2. Look for the almosts and the moments that decided the round. Read how in How to analyze a BJJ match.
  3. Pick one thing to work on. One task you will drill this week, and go over the match one by one, not with a list of ten things at the same time.
  4. Keep the footage by date. So you can come back to it in a few months and compare.

Questions people ask

Should I film my BJJ rolls?

Yes, if your academy and your partners are fine with it. Video is the best way to see what you actually do in a round, not what you remember, and it is the clearest way to see your mistakes and your progress over time.

Is it okay to film rolls at my gym?

Ask your coach first, and ask your partner before each round. Keep the footage for your own study, and do not post anyone publicly without their permission.

What is the best camera angle for filming BJJ?

From the side of the mat, a step back, a little above hip height. That keeps both athletes in frame and shows grips and hips with less blocking.

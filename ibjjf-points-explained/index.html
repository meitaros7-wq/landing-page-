IBJJF points explained: how a BJJ match is scored

In an IBJJF match you score points by reaching a dominant position, or by making a point-scoring move, and stabilizing it for three seconds: 2 points for a takedown, a sweep or knee on belly, 3 points for a guard pass, and 4 points for mount or back control. A submission ends the match whatever the score. If the points are tied, advantages decide, then penalties, then the referee.

By the Matsense team, reviewed by black belt world champion Amit Burshtein

The IBJJF point values

  • Takedown: 2 points. Taking your opponent down from standing and establishing the top position.
  • Sweep: 2 points. Starting from guard, reversing the position and ending up on top.
  • Knee on belly: 2 points. Knee on the opponent’s belly or chest, with the other leg posted, from the top.
  • Guard pass: 3 points. Getting past the opponent’s legs and hip line and establishing control in side control or north-south.
  • Mount: 4 points. Sitting on the opponent’s torso, facing their head. Mount from the back counts too.
  • Back control: 4 points. Behind the opponent with both hooks in.

The 3-second rule

A position scores only when it is held for three seconds. If you pass the guard and your opponent recovers guard after two seconds, the pass scores nothing. That is why experienced competitors stabilize first and only then move on to the next position or the submission.

You can also score points simultaneously, if you make a point-scoring move and stabilize it for at least three seconds. Pass the guard straight to mount, and you get 3 points for the pass and 4 for the mount.

Points are given for positions, not for effort. A beautiful attempt that does not finish in a scoring position held for three seconds gives no points. It may give an advantage, if the position would have scored but was not held long enough.

Advantages: the almosts on the scoreboard

An advantage is given when an athlete reaches a point-scoring position and does not hold it for three seconds, or puts the opponent in real danger of a submission without completing it. Advantages are not added to the points. They only decide the match when the points are tied.

Advantages are the official version of the almosts. A match you lost on advantages is a match where one almost, finished, would have changed the result. Read more in How to analyze a BJJ match.

Penalties

Penalties are given for stalling, for leaving the mat to escape a position, and for other rule violations. They escalate: the first is just a penalty, the second gives your opponent an advantage, the third gives them 2 points, and the fourth disqualifies you. Some violations, like an illegal technique for your belt, disqualify you at once.

How a match without a submission is decided

  1. Points. The athlete with more points wins.
  2. Advantages. If the points are tied, more advantages wins.
  3. Penalties. If both are tied, fewer penalties wins.
  4. Referee decision. If everything is tied, the referee chooses the athlete they think deserves the win, by their own judgement.

How competitors use the scoring rules

  • Know the score at every moment. When you are ahead late in a match, safe control matters more than a risky attack.
  • Stabilize before you move on. Three seconds on top is worth more than a fast transition that scores nothing.
  • Count the advantages in close matches. With the points tied, one more near pass or real submission threat can decide it.

How to scout a BJJ opponent

3 min read7 sections
The short answer

To scout a BJJ opponent, find two or three of their matches and look for patterns: how they like to start, where they score, which submissions they hit, and where they get stuck. Then prepare one or two small adjustments to your own game plan. Scouting is about knowing what you will face, not about changing who you are as a competitor.

By the Matsense team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Find out who you might face

Most tournaments publish brackets a few days before the event. Check your division, note the names, and see who you are likely to meet in the first rounds.

Find their footage

  • Event streams and replays. Many large tournaments are streamed, and past matches are often available in the replay.
  • Their own channels. Athletes and academies often post matches on social media and YouTube.
  • Past results. Event results show who they beat and lost to, and how often they win by submission.
  • Your teammates. Someone on your team may have competed against them or rolled with them before.

What to look for

  • The start. Do they pull guard or go for the takedown? Which grips do they look for first?
  • On top. Their passing style: pressure, speed, or a mix.
  • On the bottom. Their guard, and the sweep or the submission they want from it.
  • The finish. The submissions they hit most often, and from where.
  • Where they get stuck. The positions where they lose points or stall.
  • The score. How they behave when they are ahead, and when they are behind.

Watch each match the same way you would watch your own: the moments that decided it, and the almosts. Read how in How to analyze a BJJ match.

Look for patterns, not one match

One match can mislead you. Their opponent may have forced a different game. When the same start, the same pass or the same submission shows up in two or three matches, that is a real habit you can prepare for.

Keep it in proportion

Scouting should make you calmer, not more nervous. Your opponent may also change their game on the day. Use what you learn as a small advantage, to be less in the unknown, and put most of your focus on your own game.

Questions people ask

How do you scout an opponent in BJJ?

Find two or three of their matches, and look for patterns: how they start, where they score, which submissions they hit and where they get stuck. Then prepare adjustments in your own game plan.

Where can I find footage of my BJJ opponent?

Look at event streams and replays, the athlete's and their academy's social media and YouTube channels, and ask teammates who have competed against them.

Should I change my game plan for one opponent?

No. Keep your main game plan, and know what to expect and what to be aware of, and only make small adjustments, such as your answer to their start and how to avoid their best position.

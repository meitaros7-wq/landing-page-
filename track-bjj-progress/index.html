How to track your BJJ progress

3 min read8 sections
The short answer

Progress in BJJ is hard to feel from day to day, because your training partners improve with you. To see it, track three things: a short training journal after each session, footage of your rolls and matches over time, and the patterns across them, especially the almosts that keep coming back. When the same almost starts to finish, that is progress you can see.

By the Matsense team, built and tested by IBJJF world champions

Why progress is hard to see

  • Your partners improve too. The rounds feel just as hard, even when you are better.
  • You roll with tougher people. As you improve, you choose harder rounds.
  • Belts and stripes are slow. They come months or years apart, and say little about what changed this month.

Keep a short training journal

Write three to five lines after each session, while you still remember. Short notes you actually write are worth more than long notes you skip.

Training journal
  • What was taught, in one line.
  • What worked in rolling.
  • One problem that came up, and in which position.
  • One question for your coach.

Film regularly and compare

Film a few rounds every few weeks, and keep them by date. Every few months, watch an old round next to a new one, ideally from the same position or against a similar partner. Read how in How to film your BJJ rolls.

Track the patterns, not every detail

The most useful thing to track is what repeats:

  • The same almost across matches. The sweep that keeps almost working, or the pass you keep almost stopping.
  • The positions where you get stuck.
  • Where you score, and from which positions.

You are looking for direction, not exact numbers: is the same problem showing up less often than it did two months ago?

Work on one thing at a time

Turn the biggest pattern into one task, drill it from the exact position, and test it in positional sparring and in live rounds. When it starts to work, move to the next one. Read more in How to analyze a BJJ match.

Questions people ask

How do I know if I am improving at BJJ?

Compare footage of your rolls from a few months apart, and check whether the problems you wrote down in your journal show up less often. Surviving longer in bad positions and finishing an almost that used to slip away are clear signs.

What should I write in a BJJ training journal?

Keep it short: what was taught, what worked in rolling, one problem that came up and in which position, and one question for your coach.

How often should I film my BJJ rolls?

A few rounds every few weeks is enough to see changes over time. Keep the footage by date so you can compare old and new rounds.
